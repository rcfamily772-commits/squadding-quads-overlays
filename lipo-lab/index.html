Why battery weight matters

A bigger pack stores more energy, but the motors must lift more mass. Ideal induced hover power scales roughly as mass to the power of 1.5. Actual FPV power also depends on drag, propellers, manoeuvres, motor efficiency, voltage sag and pilot behaviour. Lighter packs can have less energy and may struggle to deliver high current; heavier packs can increase momentum, braking distance and crash energy.

Model, assumptions and research

Time = reference time × (capacity / reference capacity) × (reference takeoff mass / candidate takeoff mass)^exponent. Nominal energy = 22.2 × capacity / 1000 Wh. Static thrust-to-weight = 4 × static thrust per motor / takeoff mass. Assumes similar usable capacity fraction and energy delivery across packs, constant voltage class, unchanged airframe and identical flight style. It does not model current limits or voltage sag.

Default pack masses (170, 209, 239, 299, 340g) are preliminary SQ working estimates from the design research, not comprehensive market averages or individual verified product specifications. See Rotorama independent 6S battery benchmark for examples of measured weights and discharge performance. Weigh your actual pack.

The Mario preset uses a provisional 493g dry weight and 1,337g per-motor static thrust proxy from earlier design research; neither is a measured Mario 5 White result. The earlier Mario 5 DC/XH O4 manual specifies 1300–1500mAh and a different dry weight/prop configuration. Confirm your exact revision, dimensions, current and mounting limits. The default six-minute reference is illustrative.

Never use predictions as a flight timer or safety limit. Observe actual voltage and current, land with an appropriate reserve, and follow airframe and battery manufacturer limits. SQ Lab field validation is pending.
